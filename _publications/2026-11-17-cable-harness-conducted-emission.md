Authors: **R. Pal**, H. Muniganti, D. Gope, and R. Krishnan

DOI: [10.5281/zenodo.24303950](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24303950)

Abstract:
Vehicle electronic components are qualified for conducted emission (CE) one at a time, on a standard test bench, according to CISPR 25. Many emission problems nevertheless appear only when the components are connected through the vehicle harness, at a stage where a correction is expensive. The vehicle manufacturer, who integrates the system, usually has no access to the suppliers' board designs and cannot simulate these problems in advance. This paper reports an industrial study of CE prediction at the integration level, in which the harness is the central element. The harness is imported from its design description and modelled as a multiconductor transmission-line network, whose parameters come from two-dimensional cross-section solutions. The network is solved together with the line impedance stabilisation network (LISN) and with black-box models of the supplier electronics. The range of validity of the transmission-line model was established by comparison with three-dimensional full-wave solutions for wires at different heights above a reference plane. The predicted emission was then compared with an in-house bench measurement of a switching noise source feeding a two-wire harness. The results show that the transmission-line model is adequate over the CE frequency band, and that the complete flow predicts the harmonic peaks of the measured emission, the dominant low-order harmonics within a few decibels.
